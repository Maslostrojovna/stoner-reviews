
# Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas (1998)

**Watch time:** 1h 58m

*Come for the reptiles. Stay for the revelations.*

## Short verdict

Carpets breathe, casino guests turn into reptiles, and Johnny Depp somehow makes paranoia hilarious. A gloriously deranged ride with a surprising amount going on upstairs.

## Review

Somewhere in Las Vegas, a man checks into a hotel. The carpet starts breathing, the guests turn into reptiles, and the whole place begins to look like a particularly unpleasant zoo. The man, a journalist named Raoul Duke, is understandably concerned. Nobody else seems to have noticed.

Mr. Duke (Johnny Depp) is accompanied by his attorney Dr. Gonzo (Benicio Del Toro). Together they drive to Las Vegas in a red convertible to cover an off-road motorcycle race. They bring a suitcase full of recreational pharmaceuticals and an unwavering dedication to Gonzo journalism.

### Why watch it high?

Terry Gilliam makes sure you're along for the ride. The camera sways, perspectives distort, and perfectly ordinary conversations become strangely threatening. For someone who claims never to have taken LSD, Gilliam does an amazing job of recreating the trip, much like Hunter S. Thompson's original Gonzo journalism. You're inside Duke's head, where even the stupidest decisions follow a strangely convincing logic.

Watching high makes the visuals wonderfully absorbing and the absurd humour even funnier. Depp's mumbling, paranoid monologues are worth the admission alone.

There's a clever satire underneath, too. Vegas is a place where money, intimidation, a pair of balls and a convincing lawyer can get you just about anything. The American Dream reincarnated!

Besides, how many movies can make you laugh uncontrollably at a pair of drug-addled lunatics and then leave you contemplating the collapse of an entire generation's ideals?

Fair warning, though: things get pretty intense. If you're prone to anxious highs, perhaps save this one for another night.
